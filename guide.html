Guide

Three keys, one number, and a list that stays on your device. Here is exactly how it behaves.

Start and stop

  1. Hold space. The display flashes 00 in a different colour — that means the timer is armed but not yet running.
  2. Release space to start counting. The digits begin moving immediately.
  3. Press any key to stop. The time is appended to the list on the left.

On a phone or tablet, press and hold anywhere on the timer area instead, and lift your finger to start. Tap again to stop.

The hold step exists on purpose: it stops a stray key press from starting a run, and it gives your hands a moment to settle before the clock moves.

Reading the numbers

Time
0.000
Average of 5
Your last five solves, with the fastest and slowest thrown away; the middle three are averaged.
Average of 12
The same idea over the last twelve — drop one at each end, average the remaining ten.
Best time
The single fastest solve in the whole list, shown in green.

Dropping the extremes means one lucky or one ruined solve does not drag the average around. Averages appear only once there are enough solves to fill the window.

Where your times live

Everything is stored in this browser, on this device, under the key hold-timer.solves.v1. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account, so clearing site data, using a private window, or opening the page from a different address starts you with an empty list.

Clear times at the bottom of the sidebar wipes the list after a confirmation. There is no undo, because there is no copy anywhere else.

Small details

The clock is measured with performance.now(), so a system clock adjustment mid-solve does not affect the result. Times are recorded to the millisecond and shown to three decimals; past one minute they switch to m:ss.mmm.

Browser shortcuts are left alone — the timer only takes over space itself, and stops on another key only while a solve is running.
